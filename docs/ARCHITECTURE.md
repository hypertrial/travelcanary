# Architecture

## Isolated local conditions

Private ingestion state V14 retains transport-owned alerts, conditions, quotas and leases, collection catalog/revision, expanded source receipts and the dual-publication transition. Collection defaults to catalog 2 with Snapshot V10 and Conditions V2; explicitly activated catalog 3 uses Snapshot V11 and Conditions V3. Historical V1–V13 readers remain frozen; the first migrated write preserves an immutable backup of the actual previous wire version. V11 Digitraffic disruptions still migrate deterministically to infrastructure incidents. Unknown legacy alert transport ownership remains unassigned rather than guessed.

`/api/cron/conditions` runs at minute 27 hourly. It does not publish alerts. Native bounded fetches use fixed HTTPS hosts, no redirects, four-second request timeouts, eight concurrent tasks, 128 requests and 16 MiB total transfer within the 45-second source phase. Infrastructure has an additional shared 64-request / 8-MiB ceiling and eight-second per-source budget. Enabled infrastructure transports are Digitraffic (FI), Krisinformation (SE), NDW safety/closure DATEX snapshots (NL), up to 24 reviewed Autobahn IDs and 48 endpoint reads (DE), and PSE use-advisory states 2/3 (PL). EAC and Enemalta parsing remain gated after the former missed the live deadline and the latter's complete planned list exceeded 512 KiB. Complete healthy responses replace only that source; partial responses merge refreshed records with unexpired prior records; failures retain only unexpired records. No infrastructure health enters alert health.

The worker claims a 90-second compare-and-swap lease and persists weighted reservations before requests. Reservations count conservatively through the source deadline and enforce shared rolling 400/minute, 2,000/hour, 8,000/day Open-Meteo limits, including failed requests. Forecast work is ordered by missing/expired, expiry before the next hourly pass plus 15 minutes, normally due, oldest attempt, sparse-island priority, and stable destination ID. A failed batch may be split once into two sequential halves only for a batch-level transport, JSON, or cardinality failure. The duplicate location weight is reserved atomically under the active lease before either retry request; rate limits, cooldown, insufficient quota, request/byte headroom, or deadline suppress the split. Valid rows remain isolated and never cause successful peers to be retried.

A failed forecast location's cadence marker is removed during the compare-and-swap merge only when it still belongs to that run, making it due on the next pass without refunding quota or disturbing successful peers. HTTP 429 establishes persisted backoff; concurrent responses retain the longest bounded Retry-After, never shorten an existing run's cooldown. Results merge into freshly read state so concurrent alert changes survive. Autobahn records a bounded attempt key for every reviewed road/type pair: failures have no completed timestamp and therefore sort before the oldest successful targets on the next hourly pass. Partial responses preserve refreshed and still-current prior records; a complete response performs source replacement. Country publication attempts all 28 catalog-2 or 45 catalog-3 files with four-way concurrency; the transition publishes both families from the same evidence before acknowledgment and throughout the subsequent 24-hour window. Each family publishes monotonically under ETags; one country failure cannot stop its siblings. Every newer generation is written even when its records are otherwise identical, keeping `generatedAt` an honest hourly publication heartbeat; ordinary catalog-2 publishing reserves `unchanged` for a stored generation at least as new as the candidate. Operational catalog-3 publication, including its dual catalog-2 outputs, requires exact candidate agreement: newer or equal-time different content is a concurrent-update failure, not acknowledgment. The route reports published, unchanged and failed counts plus fixed-code forecast and infrastructure diagnostics, capped deterministic examples, and no raw exception, URL, source prose, or destination ID. Its lease remains held through publication and is released in a `finally` path, so the next scheduled or operator-triggered pass can retry every failed country immediately.

Conditions V2 country files contain exact country catalog IDs, producer SHA, controlled limitations, a bounded attribution dictionary, country-scoped source health, forecasts/observations, up to three ranked infrastructure incidents per destination, and one national system condition. Aggregate Open-Meteo and MET Norway health stays private because one failed batch or fallback cannot identify every country as failed; public forecast completeness comes from each destination's current records, timestamps, and limitation codes. An `ok` nonforecast source with no current records is healthy-empty, not unavailable. Active precedes planned, utilities precede roads, and resolved records are filtered before every publication. The verifier blocks a record already expired at the file's generation time, tolerates expected browser-hidden expiry for 75 minutes after generation, and warns only when cleanup is overdue after that publication window. Maximum 25 forecast samples and three local records per list; 128 KiB/country and 1.5 MiB total. Optional cache payload has a 2 MiB sublimit and is evicted oldest-first before the 5 MB state hard limit. Active alerts and quota/lease records are never evicted for conditions.

The selected briefing is keyed by destination ID, resetting native disclosures and scroll state on direct map/attention selection without resetting the shared country cache. Its sticky header is opaque so scrolled body text cannot bleed into the destination name or risk result. After any hazard cards, it renders the compact freshness/coverage summary, Local conditions, and then the detailed monitoring sections; compact limitation chips keep gaps visible before conditions without forcing the full source inventory above them. Conditions failures expose a local retry control; retries reuse bounded validation, request deduplication and obsolete-response guards and never call the alert loader. Pending retries disable the control. On successful recovery, focus returns to the conditions heading after the focused retry control is removed. The browser derives missing expected weather, modeled-air-quality, and supported marine labels from current aged records. Legacy marine eligibility comes from the committed 131-destination offshore-cell artifact; 21 legacy coastal product exclusions carry `outside-product`, are not shown as transient failures, and are never scheduled for retries.

The hourly conditions worker also performs one bounded IPMA station request and two bounded regional seismic requests when due. Station observations replace only IPMA observation records at 20 reviewed destinations; broad regional destinations such as the Algarve are excluded from point-station representation. The Azores and mainland/Madeira seismic feeds replace only IPMA earthquake context after a successful parse; filtering is fixed at the previous 24 hours, magnitude 3 or greater, within 100 km, and three records per destination. These records never enter alert scoring, coverage, or global source health.

The lazy destination chunk fetches `conditions/v2/CC.json` for catalog 2 or `catalogs/3/conditions/v3/CC.json` for catalog 3 from the approved Blob namespace (local fixtures in demo). A four-entry, five-minute promise cache deduplicates country requests; navigation cleanup ignores obsolete responses. Six-second request expiry, bounded streaming, schema/catalog validation and browser aging isolate malformed, stale or unavailable conditions from the map. No V1 fallback exists and no conditions fetch occurs before selection or when the conditions switch is off.

TravelCanary (`travelcanary.org`) is one static-first Next.js application on Vercel. Normal page views download static assets, `locations.json`, one public Blob snapshot, and OpenFreeMap tiles. They never invoke ingestion or an upstream hazard source.

```text
MeteoAlarm + EEA AQI   ┐
USGS + EMSC            │
CEMS + GDACS + EFFIS   │
Vigicrues + FOEN       ├─ Vercel cron ─ normalize/match/rank ─ private state Blob
LHP + ten national partitions                               └ public latest/previous JSON

```

## Data boundaries

- `data/locations.json` is the frozen legacy server-side matching catalog; catalog 3 extends it through the reviewed release roster while preserving every legacy ID. `public/locations.json` omits geometry, source mappings, and provenance to keep browser transfer small.
- `data/coverage.json` is the country-by-hazard and location-override source for coverage. A browser-safe presentation module combines it with the canonical provider registry, destination type, public provider health, and location state; the browser makes no additional coverage request.
- When every weather subtype has the same incomplete status, the public snapshot groups flood, heat/cold, snow/ice, avalanche, and coastal-warning gaps under the `severe-weather` umbrella. This keeps the 503-location snapshot bounded without implying that those subtypes are complete.
- `ingestion-state.json` is private schema V14. Bounded events, candidates, coverage, fingerprints, conditions, attempts, quotas and leases survive migration. Captured collection/revision fences reject stale source and conditions work; cancelled forecast callbacks drain before owned-lease cleanup, retaining real throttle cooldown. Blob CAS uses independently captured control metadata because workers may mutate the read data. Catalog 3 collection requires the drained forward-only activation command; stale collection revisions cannot write results.
- Explicit MeteoAlarm CAP update/cancellation references from the primary feed or IFRC supersede matching warnings within the same country across delivery paths, including legacy records with unassigned transport ownership. They are applied after transport replacement so another delivery cannot reinsert a superseded record in the same result. Broad national-fallback list-replacement prefixes remain transport-local; failed, not-due, and obsolete results cannot issue cross-transport removals.
- `latest.json` and `previous.json` are public, sanitized Snapshot V10/catalog V2 files for exactly 503 configured destinations. Sanitized transport health exposes role, status, update time, limitation, and official URL but never endpoints or credentials. V1–V9 remain readable; older 500-destination snapshots synthesize the three Azores destinations as update-pending. The first V10 write preserves immutable `snapshot-v9-backup.json`.
- `data/hazard-applicability.json` is an offline-generated, reviewed artifact. It records the GVP Holocene WFS version and digest, applicable destination IDs, contributing volcano IDs, minimum geometry distance, and explicit overrides. Runtime ingestion never fetches GVP. Volcanic activity applies within 200 km or through an override; a current matched volcano incident always restores applicability for that snapshot.
- Zod validates every boundary. Raw upstream payloads and free-form HTML are never published.
- Compatible catalog-3 readers use explicit Snapshot V11 and Conditions V3 contracts with exact release membership. They retain the actual version of an older snapshot and show missing destinations as unknown/update-pending with unavailable freshness. Conditions and request state are isolated by catalog release; the build defaults to catalog 2, while catalog 3 requires explicit configuration and verified versioned publication.
- The lightweight application shell starts catalog and snapshot requests before dynamically loading MapLibre. Critical destination briefings ship with the shell so selection immediately opens accessible details. Detailed monitoring information, local conditions, and the failure-only text directory remain separate client chunks loaded only when selected or needed. Context loading has a visible status and isolated reload recovery; destination alerts, evidence, close controls, and navigation remain available if that code fails. MapLibre 6's module worker is copied from the pinned package into a same-origin generated public asset during install/build, then configured explicitly before map initialization. A module or tile failure falls back to the searchable text directory; mobile changes to the Alerts/list surface while retaining global search and retry.
- The responsive shell is a 344-pixel rail/map workspace at 1024 pixels and above, widening to 368 pixels at 1360 pixels, and a safe-area-aware Map/Alerts app shell below 768 pixels (plus coarse-pointer short landscape). The desktop rail uses one-line status presentation and section-level layout rather than nested floating cards: search, three visible count-aligned filters, secondary full-catalog navigation, and a footer attention action with separate total and strongest-action copy. Its complete contents remain one independently scrollable flow on short screens. Search, attention, and app-menu popovers share bounded surface and row rules while compact mobile sheets retain their own presentation. At 1360 pixels, destination details become a 424-pixel third column; narrower desktop details are a right overlay. Mobile keeps the map mounted under tab changes, uses full-height search, and renders destination details as a 44dvh/92dvh modal sheet with local peek/expanded height state. Pointer movement updates the sheet height live before the gesture snaps or dismisses; pointer cancellation restores the prior snap point. The Alerts badge and heading are derived from the active filter's count so unavailable-update rows remain distinct from alert destinations. The parent keeps the briefing open and disables search and Clear until app-initiated Back completes. Duplicate Close and intervening selection are ignored during that transition; only then are selection/query cleared and focus restored. Direct-link closure uses synchronous URL replacement. Forward retains its destination entry. Closing mobile details restores search-origin or fallback focus to the collapsed search trigger, keeping the map uncovered for the next drag; desktop input restoration and other originating controls remain intact.
- Hosted Positron remains the source style; an allowlisted, failure-tolerant paint pass and same-origin country fill remain cosmetic. The opening camera fits fixed reviewed core bounds containing 496 current destinations. `core`, `all-coverage`, `manual`, and `destination` modes govern only presentation. In catalog 2, seven outer destinations remain searchable and available through a computed all-catalog action. Selection captures the prior center/zoom/mode once; closing restores it with newly calculated padding. Overview fits track actual container dimensions as well as window dimensions so responsive layout changes cannot reuse a stale fit. Manual navigation remains authoritative across filters, refreshes, health, tab, and responsive changes.
- The alert GeoJSON source clusters through zoom 6 with a 46-pixel radius and aggregates maximum severity. Cluster count and severity are both rendered; activation uses MapLibre's expansion zoom. A separate source renders the selected location. Custom camera controls use 0.75-level steps, 220 ms transitions (zero under reduced motion), zoom limits 1.8/12, and no pan bounds or world copies.
- `map-presentation.ts` defines marker eligibility and destination-level filter counts from the same aged snapshot. `MapFilters` renders native pressed-state buttons and qualified empty results, including a switch back to Be aware destinations when High & Severe is empty. Missing snapshot/catalog data has unknown counts, never a zero-alert assertion. Filter changes update the existing GeoJSON source without upstream requests, scoring changes, or camera resets. Search and the attention list are independent of map filtering. Context evidence retains its original role and cannot establish monitoring completeness.
- The public header mark is a compressed PNG of the TravelCanary pin and canary. The favicon and Open Graph image are App Router metadata files. Brand accents use navy and gold sampled from the mark; risk colors remain independent semantic tokens.
- URL synchronization validates `destination`, `view`, and `filter` after the catalog loads. Selection pushes history (deduplicated for WebKit's repeated selection callback), tab/filter changes replace, and closing uses Back for an in-app selection after first removing the current destination parameter. The root `not-found` page provides an accessible route back to search.
- `manifest.ts`, regular/maskable icons, Apple metadata, and `viewport-fit=cover` enable standalone installation. The design intentionally registers no service worker and creates no offline cache. Runtime connectivity is presentation-only and cannot change risk, coverage, or Conditions V2.
- The initial application shell uses the native system UI stack and downloads no font. The fixed Newsreader 600 Latin/Latin Extended face uses `next/font` with preloading disabled and is fetched only when a destination or major editorial state makes it necessary. The asset check requires both catalog and snapshot requests to be observed before MapLibre starts, then records map-ready and selected-destination checkpoints.

## Update behavior

The fast cron checks MeteoAlarm, USGS, EMSC fallback, CEMS, Vigicrues, and national alerts every ten minutes. Austria, Czechia, Germany, Spain, France, Italy, Luxembourg, Poland, and Sweden are enabled. Czech bulletin matching uses exact generated RÚIAN ORP intersections and separate station evidence. Poland stays partial with reviewed stations and bulletin tokens because its official current-warning transport did not provide a stable documented exact geometry contract. Station and bulletin transports fail independently. The slow cron adds a single-attempt, four-second GDACS discovery check, plus GDELT, EFFIS fire danger, EEA AQI, EFFIS/FIRMS active fire, FOEN, eHYD, SLF, licensed EAWS bulletins, EONET, and FCDO hourly. GDACS failure remains non-blocking and retains unexpired discovery candidates. The daily cron calls only EDO. Its source-specific path uses one eight-second configuration request and one 15-second WCS request rather than retrying the same slow 2.1 MB raster under the shared five-second timeout; the 3 MB limit and exact grid/class/date validation remain unchanged. The satellite cron calls only GFM every two hours. Exact-true switches independently gate IFRC fallback, EFFIS perimeters, GDELT, GloFAS targeting, GFM, and the existing context group.

EEA samples the 498 reviewed destinations in batches of 250 and uses the worst official category across curated points. The three Azores and two Canary destinations are excluded from EEA requests and health until reviewed official observation contracts are approved; the dormant Canary parser remains fail closed for future review. Active fire confirms at most 50 keyless EFFIS candidates and merges the optional fixed NOAA-20, NOAA-21, and MODIS FIRMS datasets. Vigicrues and eHYD use committed offline intersections. FOEN decodes one national palette TIFF. SLF intersects geometry directly; Avalanche.report uses reviewed EAWS region-prefix mappings and a 9 MB run budget. Successful scopes replace only their evidence; unavailable scopes retain unexpired evidence. EFFIS fire-danger health is destination-scoped: repeated no-data samples delay only affected outdoor destinations, not global health or permanent coverage. Previously checked scopes do not exempt a source that subsequently misses its cadence. A new event alone does not establish a successful scope. Explicit per-alert cancellation, all-clear, and expiry records still remove the corresponding prior evidence during a partial refresh. Context, discovery, GFM, and complementary CEMS Rapid Mapping incompleteness never create global delay.

## Coverage presentation

The canonical provider registry owns display name, official homepage, role, cadence, hazard support, and reviewed limitation copy. Server risk policy and browser coverage derivation both consume it, including CEMS flood applicability. This prevents provider health and displayed coverage from using divergent metadata.

For each applicable hazard subcheck, the browser derives an independent `coverageStatus` (`available`, `limited`, or `not_monitored`) and `freshnessStatus` (`current` or `delayed`). A category may therefore appear in both Update problems and Monitoring gaps. The presentation layer translates these to “Fully checked,” “Partly checked,” “Update delayed,” and “Not checked,” exposes every applicable hazard subcheck, and splits categories into delayed, gaps, and fully checked collections. Monitoring counts use permanent completeness only and never count a temporary delay as a gap.

Authoritative and normal complementary providers determine live coverage; fallback, discovery, disabled, GFM, EONET, EDO, FCDO, EMSC fallback, GDELT, and EFFIS/FIRMS active-fire evidence cannot satisfy it. Applicable non-covering providers with declared hazard support are derived into a separate context-provider collection and are excluded from category counts and subchecks. A national partition can determine only the hazards declared by its enabled country manifest entry. Snapshot age above 30 minutes marks normally live checks delayed; the two-hour rule still turns an otherwise-normal location into `UNKNOWN`. Aging adds applicable hazards to `delayedHazards` without changing `coverageGaps`. Static limitations remain in details and never alter map markers.

USGS geographically prefilters the daily feed, then processes qualifying event detail chains with at most three candidates in flight. Detail and ShakeMap grid URLs are fetched only when their HTTPS hostname is on the fixed USGS allowlist. USGS and EMSC events retain private source IDs, epicentres, and magnitudes so fallback reconciliation requires a shared identifier or close agreement in time, distance, and magnitude; those correlation fields are omitted from public snapshots. Snapshot construction filters events once, routes location-ID events directly, limits regional matching to the relevant country, and reserves full Turf matching for point and polygon geometry.

MeteoAlarm uses the 28 actively maintained country Atom/CAP feeds from the official feed directory; the retired RSS transport, authenticated EDR API, and future MeteoGate API are not runtime dependencies. The adapter caps each country feed at 4 MB, each CAP detail at 512 KB, the combined run at 64 MB, each feed at 2,000 prioritized records, each country at 500 emitted events, and the deduplicated CAP queue at 256 URLs. Lifecycle records, updates, and higher-severity records are retained first when a feed exceeds its record limit; every overflow marks affected output partial. Atom records mix provider-specific `EMMA_ID`, current NUTS, and legacy NUTS identifiers. Matching uses reviewed exact codes plus normalized feed area labels compared with the catalog's place and GeoNames administrative labels; a conservative label-prefix fallback is allowed only for cities, capitals, and resorts. Country-wide matching is used only when the feed explicitly says the warning is country-wide. The 17 former fallback-only destinations now use reviewed Eurostat GISCO NUTS 2024 level-2 mappings; validation reports zero catalog destinations without an exact provider/NUTS code. `data/meteoalarm-capabilities.json` records every country feed and generates core weather coverage. Flood, fire, avalanche, and coastal coverage remain partial because a weather-warning feed is not comprehensive event detection. Linked CAP retrieval is restricted to the fixed MeteoAlarm HTTPS allowlist and is needed only when Atom omits references from a cancellation or an unexpired update, or an active extreme-wildfire instruction requires review.

Each ingestion run gives upstream source work 45 seconds and reserves the remaining 15 seconds of the function limit for reconciliation and publication. One shared abort signal reaches every request; GFM and CEMS also stop scheduling new targets at the deadline. Runs record bounded adapter and read/merge/build/publish timings plus private per-source request, retry, response-byte, record, target, match, overflow, controlled-category, and controlled-outcome counters. They read ETags, build and validate a complete candidate in memory, conditionally write private state, then conditionally publish the public snapshot. Versioned reads carry the original legacy JSON only when a schema backup may be required, so normal publication reuses its already-read state and snapshot instead of fetching them again. Public Blob reads obtain authoritative metadata through the Blob control plane and use an ETag-versioned content URL, preventing the 60-second public CDN cache from hiding a just-written version. `previous.json` also advances through a conditional, monotonic write so an older overlapping publisher cannot replace a newer rollback snapshot; if the rollback object is missing, the publisher recreates it from the prior validated snapshot with a create-only write and rereads after a concurrent creation. A snapshot timestamp more than five minutes in the future is replaced from current private state and is not copied into rollback storage. One bounded retry resolves concurrent cron writes and rebuilds from the latest committed private state so an overlapping public write cannot omit newer evidence. A malformed, incomplete, internally inconsistent, or oversized candidate cannot replace the last valid snapshot.

The compatibility export `src/lib/risk.ts` separates private state transitions from public snapshot projection. Current scheduled topology is validated bidirectionally: each enabled provider must have exactly one adapter with matching cadence, disabled providers must have none, and every adapter must map to exactly one provider. `npm run sources:check` enforces this invariant before regenerating source inventory.

EFFIS fire danger requests the current daily FWI slice and fails closed on stale or changed products. Active-fire discovery scans and spatially groups the complete `all.hs` raster before ranking at most 50 confirmation points by catalog proximity. It otherwise keeps at most one current hotspot per destination, prefers VIIRS over MODIS, caps hotspot evidence at `ELEVATED`, and expires it after 12 hours. With `EFFIS_PERIMETERS_ENABLED=true`, a current WMS time-slice confirmation and bounded WFS GML polygon paired with a detection no older than 12 hours may replace that destination's hotspot with `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`-confidence perimeter evidence. Malformed, stale, unconfirmed, or non-intersecting polygons retain the hotspot fallback. All satellite active-fire results are context-only. Official wildfire warnings require reviewed exact national codes, severity, lifecycle, and geometry; generic `Fire` categories are ignored. EFFIS success is healthy without FIRMS credentials; one configured transport failure is partial and total failure is failed.

Enabled GFM merges active GDACS targets with destinations selected from the official keyless GloFAS `sumAL41EGE` days-1–3 summary under one 12-target cap. GloFAS never publishes directly: evidence still requires current aligned GFM extent, likelihood of at least 70, advisory zero, and four connected cells. Resulting evidence remains `ELEVATED`, `MEDIUM` confidence, non-covering, and expires after four hours.

## Security and privacy

- Cron routes require an exact bearer secret and accept no input parameters.
- Base upstream URLs are fixed in source code. Source-linked detail requests are limited to explicit HTTPS hostname allowlists; no user-controlled URL reaches server fetches.
- Blob write tokens, the cron secret, and `FIRMS_MAP_KEY` are server-only.
- The browser stores no searches, requests no geolocation, and loads no analytics or advertising scripts.
- The content security policy permits only application assets, the configured Blob origin, and OpenFreeMap.

## Provider scope and deferred coverage

Private state V14 and release-matched public snapshots retain per-provider candidates and clustered evidence while preserving legacy readers and immutable version-matched backups. Legacy V12 projection scripts reject V13/V14. Rollback requires the compatible V14 runtime and retains the newest collection revision, evidence and quota state. After catalog-3 activation, keep a compatible catalog-3 client and disable anomalous sources independently; do not restore older private state. The [publication contract](europe-expansion/publication.md) defines the drained cutover and immutable 24-hour compatibility window.

The fast schedule also checks keyless EMSC fallback. Hourly GDACS discovery never scores risk and has one bounded transport attempt. EMSC and satellite-only evidence are capped at `ELEVATED`. SLF, Avalanche.report, Vigicrues, FOEN, and eHYD are authoritative, coverage-scoped providers. Their failure never makes unrelated destinations unknown.

National manifest V3 records every reviewed system in all 27 EU countries plus Switzerland. Readiness is derived from separate access, reuse, severity, lifecycle, geometry, and completeness gates. Ten national partitions are active, including partial Italian flood coverage from exact warning-zone intersections and Latvian hydrological warnings from polygon/municipality joins. FMI, Met Éireann, IPMA, AEMET, and DHMZ are failure-only MeteoAlarm fallbacks. Credential- and evidence-gated transports make zero requests; blocked systems remain visible as reviewed limitations. Per-country rollback uses `NATIONAL_ALERTS_DISABLED_COUNTRIES` and cannot activate a gated system.
